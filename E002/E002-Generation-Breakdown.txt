# The biggest failuries in E002

    P5 — Missing puppy
    Major failure
    Your prompt:
    "Where did my puppy go?" Sarah asked.

    Across all nine generations, the model repeatedly fails to develop a missing-puppy narrative.

    It generates unrelated characters, presents, clothing, gardens, dolls and other scenarios.

    This is concerning because the model isn't being asked to solve a complicated problem. It just needs to maintain a simple narrative premise.

    The repeated failure across nine samples makes this more meaningful than a single unlucky generation.


    P8 — Aspiring artist
    Major failure
    Your prompt:
    Amy had always dreamed of becoming a great artist.

    Most of the generations completely abandon the premise.

    The model starts talking about:
    - Boys playing in mud.
    - Trees and balls.
    - Cakes and snacks.
    - Random children and animals.
    It doesn't meaningfully develop Amy's artistic ambition.

    This is another case where the model isn't maintaining the initial narrative objective.


    P4 — Mysterious box
    Relatively strongest prompt
    This prompt performs better than the others.

    For example:
    Tim found a mysterious box under his bed. He asked his mom, "Can I open it?" His mom said, "Yes, but be careful."

    Then the box opens and produces a bird and a ball.

    It's not a particularly sophisticated story, but there is an identifiable sequence:
    1. Tim finds something.
    2. He asks permission.
    3. He opens it.
    4. Something unexpected appears.
    5. He reacts.
    The underlying story structure is much more recognisable here.

    This is the sort of behaviour I'd like to see more consistently
